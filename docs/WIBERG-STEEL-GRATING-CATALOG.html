Steel grating variants

Heavy-duty, close-mesh, and ball-proof options within welded and press-locked steel bar grating — availability and geometry per quotation.

Unsplash

Heavy-duty grating

Larger bearing bar section and/or tighter spacing for higher loads and shorter spans — same steel bar-grating families, sized for the job.

Typical use
Wheel traffic, equipment routes, maintenance bays, drum or pallet handling zones.
Why selected
Extra capacity and stiffness when standard mesh is not sufficient for specified loads.
Selection note
State design loads, wheel loads, and span; Wiberg confirms against quotation and load assumptions.

Unsplash

Close-mesh grating

Reduced clear opening between bearing bars — pitch and mesh fixed in the schedule and quotation.

Typical use
Walkways where smaller openings are preferred; schedules citing reduced aperture without solid plate.
Why selected
Finer grid for comfort, small-object retention, or specifier preference — still open-grid drainage.
Selection note
Quote required clear opening or cited clause; tighter mesh affects mass, drainage, and detailing.

Unsplash

Ball-proof grating

Mesh geometry limited so a defined test sphere cannot pass — criterion named in contract or standard, offered as quoted.

Typical use
Stadia, public decks, plant walkways, and infrastructure where ball-drop or similar rules apply.
Why selected
Meets project falling-object rules without closing the floor solid.
Selection note
Name the rule and sphere size; verify panel-by-panel on drawings — quotation line is binding.

Materials & finishes

Base metals and protective finishes for welded and press-locked steel grating — combine as specified; grades and systems are fixed on the quotation.

Unsplash

Carbon steel

Short use cases

  • General industrial platforms, walkways, and trenches when corrosion is managed by finish or environment.
  • Cost-efficient default for indoor dry service or when paired with galvanizing / paint on the order.

Selection note Name the required steel grade or accept mill standard per quotation; always state the finish (e.g. HDG, paint system) for exposed work.

Unsplash

Stainless steel

Short use cases

  • Chemical splash, food-adjacent zones, coastal or de-icing exposure — where spec calls for stainless bearing bars.
  • Architectural or hygiene-sensitive areas needing durable, cleanable surfaces.

Selection note Cite grade (e.g. 1.4301 / 304, 1.4401 / 316) and any surface requirement; stainless is not hot-dip galvanized — finish as quoted (e.g. pickled, passivated).

Unsplash

Hot-dip galvanized

Short use cases

  • Outdoor and aggressive industrial atmospheres on carbon grating: long-life zinc barrier.
  • Walkways, stair treads, and trench covers in wet or variable weather.

Selection note If the job names a galvanizing practice, write it on the RFQ; execution and venting detail follow the quoted line and approved drawings.

Unsplash

Painted / coating systems

Short use cases

  • Specifier paint systems, duplex over galvanizing, or colour coding for safety zones.
  • Controlled indoor environments where a defined organic coating is preferred over bare steel.

Selection note Send system name, primer/topcoat, DFT, and substrate (black steel vs galvanized); shop vs site touch-up must match the quotation.

Selection guide & RFQ

Left: how to choose. Right: print or copy as your enquiry cover sheet. Deeper wording lives in the technical appendix — not here.

1 · Welded vs press-locked

Welded
Go-to for most industrial jobs when the contract allows welded bar grating or says nothing about construction type.
Press-locked
Pick when the buyer’s spec names press-locked mesh — same sites as welded, different junction detail.

Still deciding? Ask for both prices. Spans, loads, and supports still need checking either way — nothing on this page replaces that.

2 · What we need to quote

Missing items mean a qualified or delayed offer:

  • Panel / layout PDF with span parallel to bearing bars
  • Mesh call-out (bar size, pitches, plain / serrated)
  • Carbon + finish, or stainless grade
  • How you define loads / deflection — your project wording, not ours on this sheet
  • Qty, where it ships, dates, and any mandatory paperwork

3 · Loads or steelwork not frozen

  • Send a clearly marked assumption pack: trial span, width, load class words, deflection ask.
  • Request optional mesh ideas against those assumptions; re-quote when supports are fixed.
  • Say what you do not know yet — avoids silent guesses.

Earlier RFQ with honest gaps beats a late change after planning starts.

Construction types

Welded vs press-locked manufacture, use, and selection basis.

Schematic product views, crossing details, and principles — not shop drawings.

Welded steel grating

Press-locked steel grating

1. Welded steel grating

Manufacturing

  • Crossings: heat + pressure (welded grid)
  • Cross bars: usually round or twisted steel
  • Bearing bars: steel flats or other agreed steel section within quoted range

Characteristics

  • Rigid joints; load sharing; visible welds at crossings
  • Suited to industrial duty, higher traffic, layouts where stiffness and direct load paths matter

Typical use

  • Plant platforms, process flooring, maintenance walkways, heavy bays; trench/pit covers where grid is welded steel

2. Press-locked steel grating

Manufacturing

  • Mechanical lock (e.g. slotted/cold-formed interlock); no fusion weld at each crossing
  • Edge finish per order

Characteristics

  • Smooth at crossings; stable pitch/bar sizes
  • Often where appearance/flatness at crossings matters — capacity/deflection still need project checks

Typical use

  • Walkways/platforms needing a cleaner grid; architecturally sensitive industrial work only after engineering sign-off (loads, spans, supports)

3. Choosing

Selection guidance
  • Welded — often for high utilisation, conventional industrial flooring, simple support detailing
  • Press-locked — often where crossing appearance or interlock without welds at each joint is specified — after spans, loads, finishes, deflection limits are fixed in the quotation

Final choice: bearing size, spacing, span, supports, finish, released load data or project calculation — not preference alone.

Design considerations

Strength, deflection, inputs, and when project engineering is required.

Structural models are indicative — engineer of record defines actions and limits.

Basis

Grating must meet strength and deflection per project requirements.

  • Loads — from specifier/contract (magnitude, type, combinations)
  • Span — unsupported distance in bearing-bar direction between designed supports (not nominal bay alone)
  • Supports — bearing length, restraint, continuity must match table assumptions or project checks
  • Without defined loads/supports — quote geometry and mass only, not structural adequacy

Minimum inputs

  • Load type (e.g. kN/m² uniform, line/point; static vs moving if relevant)
  • Span (mm) in bearing-bar direction; continuous spans or cantilevers noted
  • Support model (simple vs continuous, min bearing, perimeter support)
  • Environment and finish
  • Panel schedule: bearing-bar direction, cut-outs, adjacent openings

When to dig deeper

Unusual spans, concentrated loads (feet, wheels, plant), heavy traffic, heavy-duty actions, large cut-outs, non-standard supports → drawings, load notes, deflection limits. Quotation states accepted for manufacture vs needs further structural input (buyer / engineer of record).

Note

Section 13 data — only under its stated assumptions. No interpolation without written agreement.

Load tables & span data

Released data only; assumptions; reading sequence; non-standard conditions.

How to read released tables — still subject to revision and quotation.

Scope

  • Applies to released, maintained Wiberg data for welded and press-locked steel bar grating (incl. released variants: heavy-duty, close-mesh, ball-proof where a separate table/footnote exists)
  • Valid only at current authorised revision
  • Loads/supports/details ≠ table assumptions → quotation + technical confirmation

Table assumptions (check each release)

  • Steel grade and design basis
  • Support model (usually simple span, min bearing)
  • Load type (uniform, patch, etc.)
  • Deflection limit
  • Self-weight in tabulated actions or not
  • Temperature, factors, dynamic rules (if any)
Note

If an assumption is missing — do not use the table; request an updated extract or project calculation.

Reading sequence

  1. Construction: welded or press-locked
  2. Bearing-bar section + s₁
  3. s₂
  4. Span ∥ bearing bars (sketch)
  5. Compare design load and deflection limit
  6. Governing check may be stress or deflection
Note

Combination not in table — do not extrapolate; RFQ with drawings and load notes.

Non-standard conditions

Serrated detail, cut-outs, banding, concentrated loads, impact, fatigue, temperature, non-simple supports — outside generic tables unless a released supplement applies. Use tables as indicative until quotation or written technical note (referenced to drawings/loads) fixes the basis.

Panel execution, tolerances, identification, and documentation on the order.

Marking

Documentation

  • Label language/units: per project agreement
Note

Installation & fixings (overview)

Layout, bearing, fixing types, joints, and erection references — overview only.

Site / support concepts — details on project drawings and Wiberg shop details for the order.

Layout and schedules

  • Supports so bearing bars span between them unless structurally approved otherwise
  • Schedules must show span direction — misalignment voids catalogue load assumptions

Bearing

  • Each bearing-bar end needs adequate support contact
  • Min bearing (mm): bar depth, reaction, support edge, fixing type — on drawings or Wiberg details for the order
  • Trimmed edges reduce effective bearing

Fixing

  • Welding — shop or field to steel supports; galvanized: agreed zinc repair after site welding
  • Clips — saddle/hold-down per detail; allow thermal movement
  • Bolting — removable panels, stainless sites, or no hot work; hardware per approved detail

Joints

  • Adjacent panels: clipped, welded, or lapped on common supports as drawn
  • Joints across span: watch differential deflection — align supports/fixings per design
  • Large areas: expansion per engineer of record

Erection

Erection: approved drawings, Wiberg shop details on quotation, specs, site HSE. This section is overview only.

Grating accessories (fixings)

Clips, banding, toe plates, and selection rules versus quotation and released details.

Accessory geometry — use released clip schedule / detail for the order.

Clip and fastener types

  • Saddle clips — on bearing bar(s), bolt to flange/angle; non-welded retention, removable panels
  • Hold-down clips — downward restraint (uplift, vibration, security); layout per detail
  • Bolts/fasteners — grade, coating, thread for environment; torque/locking per detail

Banding

Perimeter flat; section vs bar depth and edge load — confirm on quotation.

Toe / kick plates

Edge strip for slide-off/visibility; height/thickness per HSE/project; coordinate with stair nosing if treads covered elsewhere.

Welded vs press-locked

  • Clip choice: bar depth, pitch, flange access, approved detail
  • Same clip families often suit both when parameters match the released clip schedule/detail for the order
  • Welded panels: often site-welded to supports with or instead of clips
  • Press-locked: may favour mechanical fixing where site welding on the walking surface is minimised — still structurally approved
Selection guidance
  • Define: uplift, traffic, removal frequency, corrosion class, substrate
  • Clip spacing: not one-size — engineer of record or Wiberg schedules
  • Avoid wrong fastener grade on stainless (bimetallic risk)
  • BOM + detail drawings with accessory line — do not assume inclusion without a listed item

Ordering, RFQ & commercial

Quotation inputs, drawings, custom work, lead time, and contract precedence.

Commercial package overview — binding terms only in contract / proforma.

Quotation inputs

  • Grating: welded or press-locked (+ variant if required)
  • Bearing bar h × t (mm); s₁, s₂ (c/c)
  • Plain or serrated
  • Panel L × W (mm) + dimension ∥ bearing bars
  • Quantity: panels, m², or mass
  • Loads (e.g. kN/m²) + deflection limit if structural check requested
  • Finish: untreated, HDG, paint system, stainless grade
  • Accessories: clips, banding, toe plates, hold-downs — quantities or per-panel rule
  • Delivery: port/country, dates, packing/marking
Note

Stair treads, trench covers, FRP: additional datasheets/RFQ lists.

Drawings

  • PDF: panel marks, grid orientation, cut-outs, support lines; DWG/DXF if available
  • Cite spec clauses: material, tolerances, welding, galvanizing, inspection
  • Name required certificates/tests up front

Custom work

  • Non-standard pitches, sizes, cut-outs, mixed finishes: shop capacity + material
  • Early RFQ with stable grid/support data reduces rework

Lead time and terms

  • Lead time: per order (workload, material, finishing, inspection, documentation)
  • Incoterms, payment, currency, taxes: contract/proforma — catalog does not set binding commercial terms
  • Quotation validity: on each offer
