INTRODUCTION
This manual summarises elastic design principles for bar grating used as simply supported flooring. Factored resistances, partial factors, and national annexes remain the responsibility of the Engineer of Record. Wiberg issues project-specific load tables and shop drawings for each order. Numeric tables in this edition are generated from master-data1.js (YB/T 4001.1 Appendix E excerpts).

SECTION PROPERTIES
For a rectangular bearing bar (depth d, thickness t), per metre width including s₁ spacing:
I ≈ (t × d³ / 12) / s₁   (m⁴ per m width)
Z ≈ I / (d/2)   (m³ per m width)
Twisted cross bars contribute to composite stiffness in welded grating; manufacturer tests calibrate effective stiffness. Use Wiberg-released section moduli for contract calculations.
Elastic bending stress
σ = M / Zeff   (uniform moment M per unit width)

DESIGN LOADS
Uniform load
w (kN/m²) over tributary width. For bar strip model, equivalent line load q = w × s₁ (kN/m per bar).
Concentrated / patch load
Replace with static equivalent or use plate distribution width beff per code (e.g. NAAMM guidance). For wheel loads, combine contact patch dimensions with span direction.
Vehicular
Define axle load, impact factor, multiple presence, and load placement that produces maximum moment in bearing bars. Heavy-duty meshes often govern on shear at bearing as well as flexure.

DEFLECTION & VIBRATION
For uniform load on a simply supported strip, central deflection δ ≈ 5 w L⁴ / (384 E I) (consistent units). Compare δ to span/200, span/250, or project limit.
Pedestrian vibration: lightweight long spans may require frequency check; provide live load density and damping assumptions to Wiberg for guidance.
